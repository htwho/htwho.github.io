## Ryugu and Bennu Notes

<div class="citation-blocks">
  <article class="citation-card">
    <p class="citation-year">Reading Note | January 2021</p>
    <h3>The Origin of Ryugu and Bennu</h3>
    <p class="citation-text">This note compares the physical properties, spectroscopy, dynamical histories, and possible parent families of the near-Earth asteroids Ryugu and Bennu, based on results from Hayabusa2, OSIRIS-REx, and related orbital-dynamics studies.</p>
  </article>
</div>

<div class="citation-blocks two-column-cards">
  <article class="citation-card">
    <p class="citation-year">Main Question</p>
    <h3>Could Ryugu and Bennu share a common origin?</h3>
    <ul class="card-list">
      <li>Both asteroids are top-shaped rubble piles influenced by the YORP effect.</li>
      <li>Both have low albedo and similar bulk density and porosity.</li>
      <li>The main difference is hydration level seen in the near-infrared spectrum.</li>
      <li>The note asks whether they could still come from related parent-family material despite these spectral differences.</li>
    </ul>
  </article>
  <article class="citation-card">
    <p class="citation-year">Key Observation</p>
    <h3>Similar structure, different hydration signatures</h3>
    <ul class="card-list">
      <li>Bennu shows a strong 2.7 μm OH absorption feature, indicating hydrated minerals.</li>
      <li>Ryugu shows a much weaker 2.7 μm hydration feature and resembles more dehydrated carbonaceous material.</li>
      <li>This suggests similar physical evolution does not necessarily imply identical thermal or alteration histories.</li>
    </ul>
  </article>
</div>

<div class="citation-blocks two-column-cards">
  <article class="citation-card">
    <p class="citation-year">Dynamical Interpretation</p>
    <h3>Migration from inner main-belt asteroid families</h3>
    <ul class="card-list">
      <li>Ryugu is discussed as likely originating from the Eulalia or Polana family.</li>
      <li>Bennu is linked most strongly to New Polana, with Eulalia as another plausible source.</li>
      <li>Yarkovsky drift and stochastic YORP evolution help move small bodies toward resonance zones such as ν6 and 3:1.</li>
      <li>Those resonances can then inject fragments into near-Earth orbits.</li>
    </ul>
  </article>
  <article class="citation-card">
    <p class="citation-year">Formation Scenario</p>
    <h3>Catastrophic disruption and thermal diversity</h3>
    <ul class="card-list">
      <li>The note highlights the idea that both asteroids may come from fragments of the same disrupted parent body.</li>
      <li>Numerical disruption models suggest top-shaped rubble piles can form during gravitational reaccumulation.</li>
      <li>Different ejection regions within the disrupted parent body could explain why Bennu remained more hydrated while Ryugu experienced stronger thermal alteration.</li>
    </ul>
  </article>
</div>

<div class="citation-blocks two-column-cards">
  <article class="citation-card">
    <p class="citation-year">Takeaway</p>
    <h3>What this note argues</h3>
    <ul class="card-list">
      <li>Ryugu and Bennu likely share important evolutionary similarities in shape, bulk properties, and dynamical delivery pathways.</li>
      <li>Their spectral differences are compatible with different thermal histories rather than completely unrelated origins.</li>
      <li>Low-albedo inner-belt families such as Eulalia and New Polana remain central candidates for their source regions.</li>
    </ul>
  </article>
