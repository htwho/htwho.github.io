### Abstract

This project examined regional gas production on comet `67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko` using nadir-pointing spectroscopic observations from the `MIRO` instrument onboard Rosetta. The analysis focused on the `H2^18O` and `CH3OH` lines observed in June, July, and August 2015, spanning the period before and during perihelion, in order to investigate how molecular production varied with solar illumination and surface location.

By deriving production-related line measurements from MIRO spectra, the study compared the behavior of water and methanol across different parts of the nucleus and evaluated whether the two major lobes of the comet shared the same volatile composition. A second goal was to examine the relation between gas activity and insolation, including how the observed line areas changed with surface continuum temperature and solar zenith angle.

The analysis showed that production rates generally increased with solar insolation, but the detailed behavior of `H2O` and `CH3OH` was not identical. Although methanol-to-water ratios could become relatively high at low continuum temperature, the two lobes converged to nearly the same `CH3OH/H2O` ratio at higher temperature, suggesting similar bulk composition. This result is consistent with the interpretation that the head and body lobes of comet 67P do not show a strong large-scale chemical difference in the measured volatile ratio.

### Research Focus

- Analyze nadir-pointing MIRO spectra of `H2^18O` and `CH3OH`
- Estimate regional production behavior around perihelion
- Compare volatile properties between the head and body lobes of comet 67P
- Investigate how gas activity varies with continuum temperature and solar illumination
- Assess whether regional outgassing patterns support chemical heterogeneity or broad compositional similarity

### Main Findings

- Both `H2O` and `CH3OH` production generally increased with increasing solar insolation
- At low continuum temperature, the `CH3OH/H2O` ratio could reach comparatively high values
- At `T > 200 K`, the head and body lobes showed nearly identical average `CH3OH/H2O` ratios
- The fitted mean values for the two lobes were approximately `0.00014` for the head and `0.00013` for the body
- The similarity of these distributions suggests that the two lobes share broadly comparable volatile composition in the measured species ratio
