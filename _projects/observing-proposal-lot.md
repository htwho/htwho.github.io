### Abstract

This observing proposal was designed for the Lulin One-meter Telescope to study dynamically unstable asteroids in the outer main asteroid belt. These objects may represent material transported from different source regions, including bodies scattered outward from the main belt and objects affected by comet-like or other chaotic dynamical pathways. Because their orbital histories may differ substantially, they provide a useful sample for investigating transport processes and compositional diversity in the outer Solar System.

The scientific goal was to carry out broadband `BVRI` photometry to constrain asteroid taxonomy and test whether the dynamically unstable population shows a bimodal color distribution. In the proposal framework, C-type objects were expected to trace wetter, carbon-rich material from colder outer regions, whereas S-type objects were associated with silicate-rich surfaces shaped by different thermal histories. Measuring the relative abundance of these populations would help constrain models of orbital transport and improve our understanding of how unstable minor bodies migrate through the Solar System.

The proposal also included long-term phase-curve observations to estimate phase coefficients and deep stacked imaging to search for weak cometary activity. Target selection was guided by orbital integrations using the `Mercury` N-body code, with candidate asteroids required to show significant dynamical evolution or planetary encounters in backward integrations while remaining observationally recoverable. The observing strategy therefore combined dynamical selection with photometric characterization and activity searches.

### Observing Strategy

- Use `BVRI` broadband photometry for taxonomic classification
- Observe targets near favorable phase-angle windows whenever possible
- Apply non-sidereal tracking during asteroid imaging
- Use deep exposures under non-ideal sky conditions to maximize sensitivity to faint activity
- Co-add individual frames to search for weak cometary coma or extended emission

### Data Analysis

- Perform basic reduction and calibration with a Python-based image-processing pipeline
- Use the Pan-STARRS1 stellar catalog for photometric calibration
- Measure magnitudes in multiple bands and convert them to absolute brightness for comparison
- Compare color properties and phase behavior among dynamically unstable asteroid candidates

### Outcome

This observing effort contributed to the detection of weak cometary activity on the outer main-belt asteroid `2005 XR132`, reported on 4 May 2021. The result supported the broader motivation of the proposal by showing that some dynamically unstable outer-belt objects may preserve transitional or weakly active characteristics that are not apparent from orbital classification alone.
