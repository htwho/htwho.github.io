### Abstract

This project investigates the thermal characteristics of the Milun fault using distributed temperature sensing (DTS). The Milun fault is an active east-dipping, left-lateral reverse fault in the northern Longitudinal Valley of eastern Taiwan and has produced several damaging earthquakes in recent decades. Because temperature anomalies near active faults may reflect frictional heating, fluid migration, or lithologic contrasts, long-term thermal monitoring provides an important way to study fault-zone structure and processes.

The work is part of the Milun fault Drilling and All-inclusive Sensing Project (MiDAS), a scientific drilling program initiated in 2021 to monitor the physical properties of the fault. In MiDAS Hole-A, a distributed optical fiber system enables continuous, high-resolution temperature measurements along depth. Long-term observations since September 2022 identified a persistent but decaying thermal anomaly at approximately 540-580 m depth beneath the fault zone.

Recent analysis indicates that this anomaly was primarily associated with heat released during cementing operations in January 2022. However, the DTS record also shows that thermal decay varies with depth, with slower cooling below the fault zone. By combining cementing-related DTS observations with laboratory measurements from retrieved fault-core samples, this project evaluates the thermal diffusivity structure around Hole-A and constrains the mechanisms controlling temperature variations near the Milun fault.

### Research Focus

- Monitor long-term temperature evolution in the MiDAS Hole-A borehole using DTS
- Identify and characterize persistent thermal anomalies near and below the fault zone
- Evaluate depth-dependent thermal diffusivity using cementing-related temperature responses
- Compare in situ thermal behavior with laboratory measurements from fault-core material
- Improve understanding of heat transport, lithologic contrasts, and fault-zone properties in an active fault environment
