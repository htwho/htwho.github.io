### Abstract

This project investigated coseismic groundwater-level responses using high-frequency observations from groundwater monitoring stations in Taiwan. Groundwater levels are influenced by multiple factors, including atmospheric pressure, rainfall, tides, pumping, and earthquakes. During seismic events, crustal compression and extension can modify pore structure and pressure conditions in the subsurface, producing oscillations and step-like changes in groundwater level.

The study analyzed 49 earthquakes with magnitude greater than `6.0` between January 2007 and December 2022. Groundwater records from six stations in the Taiwan Earthquake and Geophysics Data Management System were used to examine how different earthquakes produced spatially variable hydrologic responses. The main goal was to compare groundwater-level changes among stations and evaluate their relation to epicentral distance, focal depth, and earthquake magnitude.

In addition to the multi-event analysis, the project examined the `M 6.83` earthquake that occurred on September 18, 2022, at 14:44:15 (UTC+8). This case study showed clear groundwater oscillations and step changes at all six stations, although the magnitude and direction of the response varied from site to site. The strongest drop was observed at the Donghe station (`DON`, `-10.83 cm`), while the Naba station (`NAB`) recorded a small increase (`+1.85 cm`). These differences suggest that local hydrogeologic conditions strongly influence the observed groundwater response.

### Research Focus

- Compile strong-earthquake events and 1 Hz groundwater-level records for comparison
- Identify coseismic oscillations and step changes in groundwater level
- Compare station sensitivity to earthquake magnitude, epicentral distance, and focal depth
- Evaluate spatial differences in hydrologic response among monitoring stations
- Use a representative earthquake event to investigate short-term station-specific behavior

### Main Findings

- Earthquakes within about `150 km` of a station generally produced stronger groundwater-level changes
- Shallow earthquakes with focal depths less than about `50 km` were more effective in generating noticeable responses
- The size of the groundwater step change was not strongly correlated with earthquake magnitude for events larger than about `M 6.2`
- Most observed step changes were decreases in groundwater level, although some stations showed local increases for specific earthquakes
- The overall spatial-sensitivity analysis did not show a clear relation between groundwater step size and static crustal stress-strain change; instead, the responses appear to be more closely related to seismic-wave energy and local hydrogeologic properties
