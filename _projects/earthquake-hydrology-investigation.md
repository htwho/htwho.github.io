<h3>Abstract</h3>

Various factors, including atmospheric pressure, earthquakes, tides, rainfall, and human activities such as groundwater extraction for irrigation, influence fluctuations in groundwater levels. Particularly during earthquakes, the compression and tension generated by tectonic plate movements can affect the porosity of rock layers, leading to oscillations and step changes in groundwater levels. This study analyzed 49 strong earthquakes with magnitudes greater than 6.0 between January 2007 and December 2022. Data from groundwater level measurements at six stations provided by the Taiwan Earthquake and Geophysics Data Management System were used to investigate the spatial distribution changes of groundwater levels during seismic events.

The study aimed to observe how different seismic events affect the spatial response of groundwater monitoring stations and to explore the relationship between station locations and seismic source depth, epicentral distance, and earthquake magnitude. In addition, a single strong earthquake event with magnitude 6.83 occurring at 14:44:15 on September 18, 2022 (UTC+8) was analyzed in detail to examine the time response of each groundwater monitoring station and the influence of a specific earthquake on groundwater levels at different stations.
