### Abstract

This project investigated the chemical composition of the inner coma of comet `67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko` using observations from the `MIRO` instrument onboard the Rosetta spacecraft. Rosetta’s long-term escort phase between 2014 and 2016 provided a unique opportunity to monitor how cometary gas emission evolved with illumination geometry, heliocentric distance, and seasonal changes on the nucleus.

The study focused on the distributions of `H2^18O` and `CH3OH`, two species that provide useful constraints on volatile release and compositional variability in the near-nucleus coma. By analyzing MIRO line areas before and around perihelion, the project examined how gas production changed with time and how outgassing responded to the day-night illumination contrast, represented by the solar zenith angle (`SZA`).

The results showed that both water and methanol activity generally increased as the comet approached perihelion. Spatial mapping also revealed strong regional variations across the nucleus, indicating that the volatile distribution was not uniform. These patterns suggest that the nucleus of comet 67P is compositionally heterogeneous, with some regions contributing more strongly to gas release than others. The project also compared MIRO measurements with dust outburst events identified by Rosetta OSIRIS in order to explore possible dust-gas interactions.

### Research Focus

- Analyze MIRO spectroscopic observations of the inner coma of comet 67P
- Compare the time evolution of `H2^18O` and `CH3OH` line areas around perihelion
- Investigate day-night asymmetry and solar illumination effects using `SZA`
- Map spatial variations in gas emission across the cometary surface
- Examine whether localized gas activity is related to observed dust outbursts

### Main Results

- Both `H2^18O` and `CH3OH` emissions increased as the comet approached perihelion
- Strong day-night asymmetry was present, with enhanced activity on the sunlit side
- Several active regions were repeatedly associated with elevated water emission
- The ratio between methanol and water varied across the nucleus, suggesting compositional heterogeneity
- Only a limited number of MIRO detections coincided with OSIRIS outburst timing, implying that gas and dust activity are related but not always directly coupled in the available observation geometry
