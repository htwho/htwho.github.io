<h3>Abstract</h3>

Asteroids having perihelion distance q < 1.3 AU are classified as near-Earth objects (NEOs), which are divided into different sub-groups: Vatira-class, Atira-class, Aten-class, Apollo-class, and Amor-class. 2020 AV2, the first Vatira orbiting totally inside Venus' orbit, was discovered by the Twilight project of the Zwicky Transient Facility on 2020 January 4. Following this discovery, several studies investigated the short-term orbital evolution of 2020 AV2. In this work, we assessed the long-term orbital evolution of known near-Earth objects and known Atiras under the Yarkovsky effect by using the Mercury6 N-body code.

The calculations considered not only planetary gravitational perturbation but also the non-gravitational Yarkovsky effect. Our results show that the NEO population generally has two dynamical components, one short-lived and the other long-lived. The transfer probabilities of Atira-class asteroids to Vatira-class asteroids for the first transition are approximately 13.1 +/- 0.400, 13.05 +/- 0.005, and 13.25 +/- 0.450 per cent for different values of the Yarkovsky force corresponding to obliquities of 0, 90, and 180 degrees, respectively. These results suggest that radiation forces may play an important role in the long-term evolution of this asteroid population. The statistical analysis further implies that there should be 8.14 +/- 0.133 Atira-class asteroids and 1.05 +/- 0.075 Vatira asteroids of the S-type taxonomy.
