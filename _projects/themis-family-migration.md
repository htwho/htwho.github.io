<h3>Abstract</h3>

The Themis family is one of the largest C-type asteroid families in the outer main belt. Its 4,782 identified members span semimajor axes of 3.02–3.27 au and lie close to the 9:4, 11:5, and 2:1 Jovian mean-motion resonances (MMRs). These resonances may transport asteroids onto planet-crossing orbits, but their relative efficiencies and long-term outcomes remain unclear. This study establishes a migration framework connecting Yarkovsky-driven semimajor-axis drift, resonance-induced orbital excitation, planetary close encounters, and final ejection.

To connect thermal migration with eventual removal, I integrated the family for 450 Myr with the MERCURY6 hybrid N-body integrator, including perturbations from all eight planets. The diurnal Yarkovsky effect was represented by a size-dependent semimajor-axis drift rate, assuming homogeneous C-type physical properties and isotropically distributed spin-axis orientations. This framework follows each asteroid from gradual Yarkovsky drift, through resonance capture and orbital excitation, to planetary close encounters and ejection.

Of the 4,782 simulated asteroids, 685 were ejected. A resonance influence score—combining proximity to a resonance (20%), residence time (40%), and resonance-associated eccentricity excitation (40%)—was used to identify the primary MMR governing each ejected object. The 2:1 MMR accounts for 480 ejected asteroids (70.1%), followed by the 11:5 MMR with 170 (24.8%) and the 9:4 MMR with 35 (5.1%). Weighted orbital distributions show that the 2:1 group undergoes substantially broader eccentricity and inclination spreading than the other two groups, confirming it as the dominant removal pathway.

The close-encounter histories reveal distinct transport routes. Asteroids associated with the 2:1 MMR often encounter Mars first, indicating an early transition to Mars-crossing orbits, whereas the 9:4 and 11:5 groups are linked mainly to Jupiter. Final encounters are dominated by Jupiter and Saturn, showing that giant-planet scattering controls late-stage removal. Some 2:1 objects end with encounters involving the inner planets, suggesting a possible pathway for delivering volatile-rich material from the Themis-family region toward the inner Solar System.

<h3>Dynamical Pathways</h3>

The modeled migration proceeds through six linked stages:

1. **Yarkovsky-driven migration:** thermal forces gradually change an asteroid's semimajor axis and deliver it toward an MMR.
2. **Resonance capture:** resonant forcing overtakes thermal drift, temporarily locking the semimajor axis near the resonance.
3. **MMR-controlled evolution:** eccentricity grows within all three resonances. The 2:1 MMR strongly excites both eccentricity and inclination, whereas the 9:4 and 11:5 MMRs mainly excite eccentricity.
4. **Departure from the Themis region:** once eccentricity reaches approximately 0.25 or higher, the orbit becomes unstable and is more likely to become planet-crossing.
5. **Planetary close encounters:** gravitational scattering breaks the resonant lock and produces abrupt, chaotic changes in semimajor axis. Objects associated with the 2:1 MMR commonly encounter Mars first, while the 9:4 and 11:5 groups are linked more directly to Jupiter.
6. **Planet-driven ejection:** late-stage encounters are dominated by the giant planets, especially Jupiter and Saturn; Jupiter is the principal driver of final removal.

Close-encounter statistics reveal an important distinction between the pathways. The 2:1 MMR can first inject bodies into Mars-crossing orbits before encounters progressively shift toward Jupiter. Some 2:1 objects end with encounters involving inner planets, indicating a possible route for volatile-rich material from the Themis region into the inner Solar System. By contrast, the 9:4 and 11:5 pathways remain more concentrated near the original family region and are connected primarily to Jupiter encounters.

<div class="project-panel" markdown="1">

<h3>Key Findings</h3>

- Yarkovsky drift supplies Themis-family asteroids to nearby Jovian MMRs, which then control eccentricity and inclination excitation.
- The 2:1 MMR is the dominant removal pathway, accounting for 70.1% of classified ejected asteroids and producing the broadest orbital spreading.
- The three resonances produce distinct encounter sequences: 2:1 objects often encounter Mars first, whereas 9:4 and 11:5 objects are mainly associated with Jupiter.
- Giant-planet scattering controls the final removal stage, with Jupiter playing the leading role.
- A subset of the 2:1 population may carry volatile-rich material from the outer main belt toward the inner Solar System.
