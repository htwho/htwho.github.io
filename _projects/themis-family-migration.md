<h3>Abstract</h3>

This project investigates the long-term dynamical evolution of the Themis asteroid family under the combined effects of the Yarkovsky force and planetary perturbations using 450 Myr N-body simulations. The Themis family, a carbon-rich C-type asteroid family in the outer main belt, is largely dynamically stable over long timescales, with about 75 per cent of simulated members remaining in the family region after 450 Myr of orbital evolution.

The simulations show that thermally driven semimajor-axis drift gradually transports part of the population into Jovian mean-motion resonances, especially the 2:1, 9:4, and 11:5 resonances. Among these pathways, the 2:1 resonance is the most efficient route for exciting eccentricity and inclination and driving rapid dynamical loss. About 14 per cent of simulated particles are eventually removed through planetary ejection, identifying gravitational scattering as the dominant loss mechanism, while direct collisions with planets are rare and occur almost exclusively with Jupiter.

Overall, the results indicate that Yarkovsky-driven transport can inject Themis family asteroids into resonance-dominated regions, but planetary scattering strongly limits their survival as long-lived inner Solar System objects. Jupiter therefore plays the central role in shaping the long-term dynamical evolution and loss pathways of Themis-family asteroids.
